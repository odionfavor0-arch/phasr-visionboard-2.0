# PHASR — Brand & Positioning

**Last updated:** June 2026

This document has two parts. The first is positioning, who PHASR is for and what it does, the strategic backbone. The second is brand voice, how PHASR feels and sounds when it speaks to her. Both should always sound like they came from the same person.

---

## Part One — Positioning

### Who She Is

She has tried every manifestation and self-improvement thing in the book to become who she really wants to be, and has never been able to follow through. She does a lot, and somehow still ends up with nothing to show for the year.

She is not undisciplined. She is over-equipped with tools that don't talk to each other, daily affirmations, meditation, vision board prayer, manifestation rituals, all tried in isolation, none of them holding her accountable to any of the others.

She has never known what it feels like to follow through on a plan. That is not a feature gap. That is an identity wound.

### What PHASR Does

With PHASR, she sets monthly goals, gets held accountable to them, and connects with other women chasing the same goal at the same pace.

Birds of a feather, flying together.

### Positioning Statement

PHASR is for the woman who has tried every manifestation and self-improvement thing in the book to become who she really wants to be, and has never been able to follow through. She does a lot, and somehow still ends up with nothing to show for the year. With PHASR, she sets monthly goals, gets held accountable to them, and connects with other women chasing the same goal at the same pace. Birds of a feather, flying together.

## Part Two — Brand Voice

### The Core Idea

PHASR is a guardian, not a coach. It doesn't push, perform, or hype. It recognizes.

The tone is warm, feminine, ambitious, and certain, the certainty of someone who has seen this story play out before and already believes in how it ends.

### What She Should Feel

The landing page shouldn't try to convince her PHASR works. It should make her feel like she's already known, before she's typed a single word.

The emotional target isn't excitement, it's recognition. The feeling of "oh, this is home" before "oh, this could help me."

She shouldn't feel like she's signing up for a tool. She should feel like she's finally arrived somewhere that already saw her coming.

### Voice Descriptors

- Warm, not hype
- Encouraging, never preachy
- Feminine, ambitious
- Certain, not performative
- Direct, never corporate
- Sees her, doesn't sell to her

### How This Shows Up

This voice is not just landing page copy. It's how Sage talks to her in the app, how rejection and acceptance emails sound, how social captions read, how every touchpoint feels. One voice, everywhere PHASR speaks.

---

## How the Two Parts Work Together

The positioning statement is the skeleton, who she is, what PHASR does, why it matters strategically. It's what goes in pitch decks, internal docs, and anywhere PHASR needs to explain itself clearly and quickly.

The brand voice is the skin, how all of that gets said out loud. It's what shapes actual landing page copy, Sage's dialogue, captions, emails.

Both are true at once. Both should be referenced together whenever something is written for PHASR.
