# PHASR Pre-Launch Content Pillars — First 12 Videos

Strategy: the page becomes known for understanding the problem before PHASR is
offered as the answer. Someone should land on the page and think "this keeps
talking about exactly what I'm struggling with" — repeatedly — before PHASR
ever gets pitched.

Territory is broader than "goal-setting app": confidence, dreams, self-belief,
consistency, identity, visibility, personal growth, turning intentions into
action, making progress visible. PHASR sits underneath those conversations —
content brand first, product brand second.

**Sequence matters — do not reorder or skip ahead:**
- Videos 1–4 diagnose the internal problem (confidence/clarity)
- Videos 5–8 diagnose the structural problem (fragmentation/systems)
- Videos 9–12 start introducing the gap between vision and execution
- PHASR enters naturally after 12, landing on: "You have the vision. You have
  the goals. You even know the person you want to become. The problem is the
  space between knowing and doing." That's where PHASR belongs.

**Standing rule for every video:** the visuals must demonstrate the idea, not
decorate the narration. Not "sad woman + text saying 'starting over.'" Instead:
start → interruption → missed days → abandonment → restart → loop. The viewer
understands the problem before it's explained. Every beat of a video gets a
*different* visual — never the same repeated card/template across a whole
video. Diagrams for research/mechanism points, illustrated scenes for a
person/moment, camera movement (pan/zoom on stills or layered graphics) for a
sense of motion — matched to what's being said, not decoration.

Each video needs two layers before any rendering starts: (1) the full script
as one continuous narrative a real person would say out loud — never four
stitched-together fragments — and (2) a scene plan where each beat's visual
is different and argues the idea. Script gets approved before any video build.

Status column tracks what's actually been posted (cross-check against Buffer
via `list_posts`, not this file alone, before treating something as done —
this file is a planning record, not the dedupe source of truth).

## 1. "You don't lack discipline. You lack clarity."
**Pillar:** Confidence & self-belief
**On-screen keyword:** "Clarity before consistency."
**Research grounding:** goal-setting specificity research (Locke & Latham
lineage, echoed across Atlassian/PositivePsychology/Psychology Today
coverage) — specific goals convert to action because they're measurable and
plannable; vague goals have nowhere for the brain to grab onto. Used for
phrasing only, never quoted as a stat on-screen.
**Full script (final, approved):**
> "Maybe you're not inconsistent. Maybe you're trying to work toward a goal you haven't clearly defined.
>
> 'Get healthier.' 'Be more consistent.' 'Build the business.'
>
> Those sound like goals, but none of them tell you what to actually do today.
>
> So you sit down to work on it and your brain has nothing to grab onto. No clear target means no clear next move. You drift, feel guilty, and call it a discipline problem.
>
> But here's the part nobody tells you: you can't consistently act on something you haven't clearly defined.
>
> So before you ask yourself, 'Why can't I stay consistent?'
>
> Ask: 'What exactly am I trying to make happen, and what does that look like today?'
>
> Because sometimes you don't need more discipline. You need a goal your brain knows how to move toward."
**Scene plan (5 beats, each a different visual):**
1. Messy collage — scattered goal-chips/notes/reminders, no order. Covers hook + the three vague-goal examples.
2. Decision-overload diagram — branching paths splitting in many directions. Covers "brain has nothing to grab onto... no clear next move."
3. Narrowing — the branching paths shown failing to converge (still scattered) as the drift/guilt line plays, landing on "you can't consistently act on something you haven't clearly defined."
4. Flow diagram — Vague goal → unclear action → inconsistent progress, contrasted with Clear goal → clear action → consistent progress. Covers the reframe question.
5. Final visual — one path lights up and resolves into a single clear step. The "click" moment: "you need a goal your brain knows how to move toward."

**LOCKED voiceover (do not rewrite/shorten/paraphrase, per Favour's explicit instruction):**
> "Maybe you're not inconsistent. Maybe you're trying to work toward a goal you haven't clearly defined. 'Get healthier.' 'Be more consistent.' 'Build the business.' Those sound like goals, but none of them tell you what to actually do today. So you sit down to work on it, and your brain has nothing to grab onto. No clear target means no clear next move. So you drift, feel guilty, and call it a discipline problem. It was never that. You don't need more pressure. You need to know exactly what you're working toward."
**Visual style (4th revision):** physical paper/cards with shadow+rotation fly-ins, stamps, handwriting-reveal text, diagrams that build themselves, camera push/zoom — no static slides, no full-sentence subtitles. Kept the dark/neon palette from the prior revision (not specified in this brief; flagged as an assumption to Favour). 9 timed scenes per her exact visual timeline (0:00-0:30+).

## 9. "Your vision board might be working against you."
**Pillar:** Dreams, goals & vision — important lead-in for PHASR (do not pitch yet)
**Hook:** "Your vision board looks beautiful. But what happens after you make it?"
**Talking point:** vision boards becoming passive inspiration when there's no connection between the vision and daily behavior.
**On-screen keyword:** "Is your vision board working against you?"
**Research grounding:** vividly picturing a goal as already achieved can trick
the brain into relaxing instead of acting (positive-fantasy research echoed
across Psychology Today / Inc coverage of vision-board psychology) — use for
authentic phrasing only, never quote stats/citations on-screen.
**Full script (drafted, one continuous narrative):**
> "I used to think making a vision board was the hard part. Cut the pictures out, stick them up, feel amazing for a week. Then nothing moved. Turns out staring at the life you want can trick your brain into feeling like you already have it — so it stops pushing you toward it. That's not a discipline problem. That's just what happens when there's nothing underneath the board. So I built the underneath part. Phases, not fantasy."
**Scene plan:**
1. Corkboard scene — cut-out photo shapes fly in and pin themselves onto a vision board; feels exciting/aspirational.
2. Frozen-board scene — board stays still while a calendar flips rapidly in the corner.
3. Diagram scene — Vision → Brain interprets the image → Temporary satisfaction → Motivation drops. Must be a diagram, not a text card.
4. Reveal scene — board tilts up like a flap, revealing empty space underneath.
5. Close scene — board settles onto visible phase-steps beneath it; PHASR wordmark appears.
**Status:** scripted + scene plan approved by Favour; not yet built
